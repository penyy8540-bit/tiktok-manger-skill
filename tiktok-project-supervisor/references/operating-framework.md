# Operating Framework

Use this reference for supervisor-level TikTok Shop project diagnosis, operating planning, and review.

## 1. Start With The Result

Define the result before discussing actions:

- What changed: GMV, orders, units, net profit, net margin, gross margin, channel contribution, or cost rate.
- Compared with what: prior month, prior week, campaign baseline, plan, or peer account.
- How large: absolute delta, percentage delta, and rate delta.
- Whether it matters: materiality, trend persistence, and whether the change affects cash, inventory, or strategic priority.

Good opening:

```text
May improved because loss narrowed by 39.6w while revenue grew 3.8%; the improvement came from SEA sales sites, but China HQ shared cost still kept the brand loss-making.
```

Bad opening:

```text
We should post more videos and find more creators.
```

## 2. Use A Driver Tree

For GMV:

```text
GMV = traffic x conversion rate x AOV
GMV = live GMV + short-video GMV + affiliate GMV + ad GMV + organic/search GMV
GMV = hero SKU sales + long-tail SKU sales + new SKU sales
```

For live:

```text
Live GMV = sessions x hours/session x average viewers x product clicks/viewer x purchase conversion x AOV
```

For creator:

```text
Creator GMV = active creators x content per creator x views/content x GPM
GPM = GMV per 1,000 views
```

For content:

```text
Content output -> qualified views -> product clicks -> add-to-cart -> orders -> repeat/retargeting
```

For profit:

```text
Net profit = revenue - product cost - platform fee - fulfillment - refund - ad spend - creator commission - samples - manpower - management/shared cost - other
```

Use rates:

- Gross margin
- Sales expense rate
- Ad spend rate
- Creator commission rate
- Fulfillment rate
- Refund rate
- Platform fee rate
- Management expense rate
- Net margin

## 3. Judge The Pattern

Use the four-quadrant lens:

| Pattern | Diagnosis | Supervisor focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue up, profit up | Healthy scaling or controlled efficiency gain | Add resources behind validated loops |
| Revenue up, profit down | Efficiency issue | Find the rate that worsened faster than revenue |
| Revenue down, profit down | Demand/scale issue, fixed-cost pressure | Stop loss, fix demand, reduce rigid costs |
| Revenue down, profit up | Active contraction or stop-loss | Verify whether future scale was harmed |

## 4. Separate Improvement Types

Do not confuse these:

- Healthy scaling: volume rises and unit economics hold or improve.
- Stop-loss: volume may fall, but loss narrows due to cost or bad-channel reduction.
- Efficiency repair: revenue may be flat, but margin, ROI, conversion, or fee rate improves.
- Structural burden: sales units improve but shared/headquarters/management costs keep total profit negative.
- One-time noise: disposal, write-off, campaign timing, creator pre-sale mismatch, exchange loss, or accounting timing.

## 5. TikTok Project Operating Map

Manage the project through six modules:

1. Product and offer
   - Hero SKU, SKU freshness, sellable stock, pricing, bundles, coupons, listing quality, product-market fit.
2. Content
   - Brand content, seeding content, conversion content, AI creative, account positioning, content cadence.
3. Creator/affiliate
   - KOL/KOC mix, creator quality, product fit, active rate, GPM, commission, sample ROI, creator incubation.
4. Livestream
   - Time slot, host stability, visual room, script path, product sequence, offer rhythm, live GMV/hour.
5. Ads
   - ROI, CPA, boosted content selection, campaign objective, audience overlap, retargeting.
6. Organization and resources
   - Content agency, BD team, live agency, merchandising, ERP/data, owner, cadence, KPI conflict.

## 6. New Project 90-Day Frame

Use 30/60/90 days to avoid fake scale.

### Days 0-30: Validate

Goal: prove that the project can produce qualified traffic and first purchases.

Deliverables:

- Account positioning and account matrix.
- Hero SKU and offer package.
- Product listing and cart path.
- First content angle set.
- First creator list and outreach standard.
- First live room model.
- Baseline metrics by channel.

KPI examples:

- Qualified video views.
- Product click rate.
- First creator GMV.
- Live GMV/hour.
- Ad ROI test threshold.
- Hero SKU conversion.

### Days 31-60: Scale What Works

Goal: increase repeatable output behind validated loops.

Deliverables:

- Content angle ranking and repeat plan.
- Creator tier structure and KOC incubation path.
- Live schedule and host backup system.
- Bundle/offer iteration.
- Paid amplification only on proven content.

KPI examples:

- GMV by channel.
- Creator active rate.
- GPM by creator tier.
- Live GMV/session and GMV/hour.
- Ad ROI.
- Repeatable content hit rate.

### Days 61-90: Systemize

Goal: make growth manageable and not dependent on one person, one creator, or one campaign.

Deliverables:

- Weekly operating dashboard.
- SKU replenishment and new product cadence.
- Creator pipeline and grading system.
- Live SOP at management level.
- Budget allocation rule.
- Risk list and next-quarter plan.

KPI examples:

- Channel GMV mix.
- Net margin or contribution margin.
- Stable live GMV/hour.
- Creator pool health.
- Content output and conversion.
- Inventory risk.

## 7. Stop-Loss vs Scale Decision

Use this when choosing between an old declining shop and a new launch.

Prioritize stop-loss when:

- Existing shop has clear GMV/profit leakage.
- Loss is material and fixable within 2-4 weeks.
- Activity is rising but output is falling.
- Inventory, ads, or live cost can burn cash quickly.

Prioritize launch when:

- New project has high search demand, product-market fit, and low TikTok competition.
- Existing shop is stable enough or has delegated owner.
- Launch needs early positioning and content seeding before competitors arrive.

Usually a supervisor split is:

```text
First month: 60-70% stop-loss on the declining mature shop, 30-40% build the new project foundation.
```

Change the split if data shows the old shop is structurally unfixable or the new project has urgent market timing.
